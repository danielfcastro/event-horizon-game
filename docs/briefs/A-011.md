## 2. The design rule every decision must survive (PLAN §8)

> Do not make the game a pure power fantasy. More mass should create tension:
> stronger pull, larger collision risk, slower precise movement, harder
> navigation, stability may decay. Real decisions: grow fast and risk
> instability? stay smaller and move precisely?

Input consequence: **movement is the whole verb set** — precision movement is
the "stay smaller and move precisely" side of every decision. Input must make
precise movement *possible and readable* at every mass (speed decay is A-007's;
input must not fight it with rubber-banding or snap-to-lane assists that erase
the tension).

## 3. Upstream decisions you honor, never renegotiate

**From PLAN §5.2 (Controls):** default mobile control is **direct drag** —
player drags the black hole across the screen; camera follows the hole;
optional joystick and tilt controls. Recommended ladder:
`Default: drag / Alternative: virtual joystick / Alternative: device tilt`.

**From spec §4 (approved, proposals you must confirm or amend):**
- §4.1: drag translates the hole; **no separate attract input** — the field is
  always on; absorption is positional (absorb by moving). The no-absorb-button
  scheme is a **proposal** — you must confirm it (spec §17 question 18).
- §4.2: joystick for players who dislike finger-over-target occlusion; tilt is
  a one-handed/accessibility alternative; alternatives selectable in settings.
  Dead zones, touch handling details, gesture disambiguation are yours.
- §4.3 verbs: Move / Hold (no input, field keeps pulling — enables baiting) /
  Retreat / Line up. No jump, dash, fire, aim, or absorb button in the default
  scheme; nothing may turn the scheme into an action game.
- §4.4: one-finger drag is the only required gesture; pinch/rotate are **not**
  camera controls (zoom is automatic per camera contract) — reserving them for
  settings only is a **proposal** you must confirm (question 18). No
  multi-finger gesture required for core play.

**From A-006 docs/design.md:** the default camera/scheme stays **centered on
the hole** (§2); A-006 hands A-011 "any assistive camera/tilt behavior". The
ring guarantee (§2) must hold under every control mode — no input may hide the
ring.

**From A-010 docs/ui.md (approved):** the bottom band reserves a **control
zone ≥ 180 ru wide** (bottom-left/center) — you own what lives there. Pause
button is 64 ru bottom-right, largest target on the HUD screen; chips are
non-interactive and never steal a thumb. Settings surface has a **calibration
entry point owned by A-011**. Touch floors: primary 64 ru, secondary 48 ru,
minimum 44 ru, spacing ≥ 12 ru. A-010 flagged two open questions for you:
(1) if the scheme needs a persistent top-band control it displaces the
pressure cluster — which stays is your call; (2) does the 64 ru pause button
need a hold-to-pause mis-tap guard given the ≥ 12 ru spacing rule?

**From A-007 (balance):** speed decays with mass (displayed, not renumbered);
input must not add its own speed curve — input translates intent, the sim
moves the hole.

## 6. Required content (suggested sections, keep under ~320 lines)

1. Purpose and inherited contract (the rules above, stated as honored).
2. Drag (default): finger mechanics, lift/reposition rules, occlusion
   tradeoff, behavior on 320×568 through 430×932 frames (A-010's range),
   interaction with the ring zone (finger may cover play space; the ring
   guarantee is about the camera, not the finger — state the policy).
3. Virtual joystick (alternative): stick placement in the reserved control
   zone, dead zones, recentering, why it exists (occlusion-averse players).
4. Device tilt (alternative): mapping, sensitivity, calibration step — answer
   question 17 here; one-handed/accessibility framing.
5. Gesture disambiguation: drag vs tap (menus) vs pinch/rotate — confirm or
   amend the pinch/rotate reservation, answer question 18; the pause-button
   mis-tap guard decision.
6. Input digest: what input emits for replayability (direction + rate
   intents, discrete events), determinism-friendly, no raw per-frame pointers.
7. Touch handling rules: multi-touch policy, input cancellation on pause,
   palm/edge rejection on small phones.
8. Accessibility hooks for A-012: what options A-012 will need (tilt
   calibration, one-handed mode, larger controls interplay with the control
   zone) — hooks only.
9. What this makes testable for A-013.
10. Open questions for downstream artifacts.
