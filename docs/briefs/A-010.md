## 2. The design rule every decision must survive (PLAN §8)

> Do not make the game a pure power fantasy. More mass should create tension:
> stronger pull, larger collision risk, slower precise movement, harder
> navigation, stability may decay. Players make real decisions: grow fast and
> risk instability? stay smaller and move precisely? upgrade for efficiency or
> pull? absorb large objects or avoid hazards?

The UI's job is to make that tension **legible**: the HUD must show mass
*pressure* (what the five levers are currently doing to you), not just a
number. A HUD that hides the cost of mass turns the game into a power fantasy
by omission.

## 3. Upstream anchors you must honor (A-006 docs/design.md, approved)

- **Ring guarantee (design §2):** the full event-horizon ring stays on screen
  with margin at all times; zoom never punches out. The camera contract is what
  keeps the HUD from competing with the play space. Your layout must not cover
  the ring zone.
- **Off-screen indicators (design §3.4):** semantics and cap are A-006's —
  only pulled bodies and field-threatening hazards, max 8, priority-sorted,
  edge chevrons, absorbable vs hazard carried by glyph shape (chevron vs
  bracketed X), never color. **Exact placement, size, and styling within safe
  areas belong to A-010** — that is yours to decide here.
- **Stability anchor (design §6.1):** ring fracture (smooth → broken, dashed,
  flickering) + pulse rhythm + audio creak. The stability meter must use the
  same cues: segmented geometry and tick rhythm, **not** a red-amber-green
  gradient alone. Meter layout is yours; the cue language is A-006's.
- **Minimum sizes (design §9):** ring stroke ≥ 2 px screen-space; edge
  indicators ≥ 8 px on the smallest supported phone; touch-target rules belong
  to A-010/A-011 — you set the touch-target floor here.
- **Handoff list (design §10):** A-010 owns HUD layout, mass meter and
  stability bar placement, edge indicator styling within safe areas, result-
  screen layout, minimum touch targets.

## 4. Numbers and semantics from upstream you display, never renumber

- **A-007 (balance):** timer `150 + 5n` s per level; 3-star line ≥35% timer
  remaining; combo window shrinks with mass; efficiency capped 1.00; mass
  ceiling 2^24 (16,777,216); five difficulty bands. Show these states; do not
  change values.
- **A-008 (content):** quantum mode (particles, phase lanes Solid/Sift/Phase),
  modifiers (one per level, L40 stacked pair), hazard glyph families (spiky +
  bracket/X). UI must surface quantum mode state and active modifier plainly.
- **A-009 (levels):** 40 levels / 5 worlds, unlock system, stars (0–3),
  endless depth tiers, daily/weekly modes. Result screens and mode menus follow
  this structure.

## 5. Open question you must answer (spec §17)

## 6. Scope boundaries — later artifacts own these, do not implement them

- **A-011 (input):** control schemes, tilt/joystick, no-absorb-button scheme,
  calibration. You may reserve space for controls and state minimum touch
  targets, but do not define the input scheme.
- **A-012 (accessibility):** reduced motion, high contrast, colorblind palette,
  larger controls implementations. You define the default layout and the
  hooks/settings surface; A-012 owns the option implementations. Every
  stability state must stay legible with color off, motion frozen, audio muted
  (A-006 anchor) — your default design must not make legibility depend on
  those being on.
- **A-013 (qa):** UI tests (drag on small/large phones, pause button size,
  safe areas) — list what your design makes testable, do not write tests.
- **A-005 (architecture):** render layer, pooling, draw-call budget — the UI
  must fit the pooled, tier-budgeted effects; do not propose per-element draw
  calls.
